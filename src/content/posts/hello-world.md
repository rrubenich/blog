Every programmer's first program prints the same two words. It felt right to start here the same way.

For more than ten years I've built software that other people depend on: patient communication for health systems, tools for hundreds of thousands of restaurants, and enterprise products used across Latin America. I loved that work. But somewhere along the way, most of what I learned stayed in pull requests, design docs and late-night incident channels, where nobody else could use it.

So I decided to change that. I'm taking this time to do what I love most: **build things, learn deeply, and share what I learn**. This blog is where that sharing happens.

## What I'll write about

Some of these I've worked with for years. Others I'm studying right now, and I'll be learning in public, mistakes included.

**Things I know well**

- **TypeScript, React and Node.js**: design systems, micro-frontends, real-time architecture, and making slow pages fast.
- **Healthcare software**: EHR integrations (Epic, Oracle Cerner, MEDITECH), HL7, FHIR, and what it takes to ship software that hospitals trust.
- **AI agents in production**: LangGraph and LangChain agents, testing agents in CI, and rolling them out safely with canary releases.
- **Shipping safely in live systems**: step-by-step migrations, incident response, and working directly with customers on go-lives.
- **Engineering leadership**: leading a team while staying hands-on, and AI-assisted development as an everyday tool.

**Things I'm studying now**

- **System design**: scalability, reliability, distributed-systems patterns, queues and async processing. I'm working through *System Design Interview* (volumes 1 and 2), one chapter a day.
- **Agent engineering**: tool use and MCP, Python agent services on FastAPI, and durable workflows with Temporal.
- **Machine learning fundamentals**: training a small DNA language model from scratch with Hugging Face, on a laptop.
- **A modern full-stack toolkit**: NestJS, Drizzle and PostgreSQL, Better Auth, React 19 and TanStack Query, with Docker, AWS and observability around them.

## What to expect

I'm preparing several posts on these topics. Some will be deep dives, others short notes about something that clicked. Each one will be honest about what worked, what didn't, and why.

If any of this sounds useful, I hope you'll stick around. And if you have a topic you'd like me to write about, you can find me on [GitHub](https://github.com/rrubenich) and [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rubenich).

Thanks for reading. See you in the next post.
